How it works

One sign-in, one QR code, and the wall runs itself for the rest of the party.

Sign in, get your board

Sign in once with Google and you get your own private board with a unique QR code — no setup, no separate accounts to manage for guests.

Guests scan and upload

Print the QR code or put it on a screen. Anyone can scan it and upload a photo from their phone's browser in seconds — no app, no login, no friction.

Photos develop, live

Every upload pushes instantly to the wall over Socket.IO. Project it on a big screen and watch the collage fill up as the event happens.

Features

Built for events where privacy matters as much as speed — everyone's board is their own.

🔒 Private per-user boards

Every board belongs to exactly one Google account. No one — not other users, only the owner and configured admins — can view, download, or clear another board's photos.

📱 No app for guests

Uploading only needs a phone camera and a browser. Guests never sign in and never see anyone else's board — just a simple upload page tied to your QR code.

⚡ Real-time wall

Socket.IO pushes each new photo into its owner's board the moment it's uploaded — no refreshing, no polling, just photos developing into view.

🗂️ Download or clear anytime

Grab every photo from your board as a zip after the event, or clear the board to start fresh for the next one — both a click away, both scoped to your own board.

🛠️ Admin directory

Designated admin emails get a directory of every registered board and inherit any legacy photos on first sign-in — handy for running the app for a wider organization.

🏠 Self-hosted, open source

No hosted service, no vendor lock-in, no one else's server holding your event's photos. Clone it, point it at your own Google OAuth client, and run it on your own infrastructure.

Honest answers

This is self-hosted software, not a SaaS product — here's what that actually means.

Is there a hosted version I can just sign up for?

No — Live Photo Collage isn't run as a service by anyone. You (or whoever deploys it) run your own instance, with your own Google OAuth client and your own storage. See the setup guide for what that involves.

Can guests see photos uploaded to other boards?

No. Uploads are only ever accepted into the board named by the QR code a guest scanned, and only that board's owner (or an admin) can ever view, download, or clear it.

Do guests need to install anything or make an account?

No. Uploading works from any phone's browser via the camera or photo picker — nothing to install, nothing to sign into.

Where do the photos actually live?

Wherever you deploy it. Photos are stored on local disk (date-partitioned) with ownership tracked in a SQLite database — on whatever server, container, or Kubernetes cluster you run the app on.

Why does it require Google Sign-In?

Boards are owned by accounts, so a sign-in is what makes a board "yours" and keeps it private from everyone else. Guests uploading via QR code never need to sign in at all.
